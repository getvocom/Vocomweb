section1Title: "Why fiber is hard to stock"
section1Body: "Fiber cable is not one product. Fiber count, fiber type, construction, jacket rating and outer diameter combine into thousands of variations, and a customer's route usually needs one specific combination. A shelf can hold the common ones. The quotes that are hard to win, and therefore worth winning, are the specific ones: rollable ribbon, high-count loose tube, micro cable for blown routes and plenum rated indoor cable.\n\nThose are also the specs most often quoted in months across much of the market. A customer who has been told to wait elsewhere does not need a catalog. They need a supplier who can say yes to the spec and give them a date.\n\nThat changes what a distributor needs from a supply partner. It is less about stock depth and more about how quickly a specific spec can be priced, built and delivered."
section2Title: "Start with the specs your customers cannot get"
section2Body: "The simplest way to add a fiber line is to add three specs rather than three hundred. Look at the quotes your team has lost or stalled on in the last year and group them. Most distributors find the same short list: high-count ribbon where duct space is the limit, micro cable for existing conduit, and indoor cable in the right fire rating.\n\nEach of these has a clear buyer and a clear pain. Ribbon buyers are usually building long backbone routes where a splice window is tight. Micro cable buyers are working in congested conduit. Indoor buyers are held to a rating by an inspector rather than by a preference. A distributor who can answer on those three has a reason to be called first.\n\nThe rest of the catalog can follow demand instead of leading it."
section3Title: "Build to order against a confirmed spec"
section3Body: "A build-to-order supplier works from the spec forward. The customer sends fiber count, type, construction, rating, footage and a needed-by date. The supplier returns a price and a delivery date, and cable is built against that order.\n\nThere is no stocking position behind it, and a good supplier says so plainly. Vocom, for example, holds no US stock. The standard lead time is 6 to 8 weeks from PO, expedited air freight is 2 to 3 weeks, and high-count or custom builds take up to 10 weeks. Any spec can be built to order as a custom build.\n\nTwo habits make this work well for a distributor. The first is quoting with the date, not just the price, so the customer can plan a pull schedule around it. The second is splitting large orders across ship dates, so cable lands against the customer's build phases instead of sitting in the yard for months."
section4Title: "What to put on the quote request, and where it goes wrong"
section4Body: "A complete request is short: fiber count, fiber type, construction, jacket rating, footage, needed-by date, destination and delivery terms. Leave any of those to assumption and the assumption is made by whoever builds the cable.\n\nThe mistakes are predictable. Specifying a count without the construction, which changes the diameter and the duct it fits. Leaving the jacket rating off an indoor order and discovering at inspection that it is riser rated where plenum was required. Mixing up delivery terms, so the customer expects the cable delivered with duties paid while the quote assumed it was collected at the factory gate. And sending the request late, so the lead time is already consumed before the order is placed.\n\nA short, structured request removes most of these. It also lets the quote come back the same week instead of after three rounds of clarifying questions."
